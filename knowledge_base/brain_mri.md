# Brain MRI

## What is a brain MRI
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain uses a strong magnetic field, radiofrequency pulses and a computer to produce detailed pictures of the brain. It gives clearer images of soft tissue than other methods and does not use ionizing radiation. MRI is considered the best imaging test for many brain disorders and the gold standard for detecting brain and spinal cord tumors.

## What a brain MRI is used for
Brain MRI is used to evaluate brain tumors, stroke, bleeding after head injury, multiple sclerosis, infections, epilepsy, hydrocephalus (fluid build-up in the ventricles), developmental abnormalities, movement disorders, and disorders of the pituitary gland and inner ear.

## Contrast agent (gadolinium)
Some brain MRI exams use a gadolinium-based contrast agent injected into a vein. Contrast makes certain structures and abnormal tissue, such as some tumors, appear brighter. The risk of an allergic reaction is very small. Small amounts of gadolinium can remain in the body after repeated exams. Gadolinium is usually avoided during pregnancy.

## Preparing for a brain MRI and safety
Patients should tell their doctor about implants, medical devices, recent surgery, allergies and possible pregnancy. Metal objects and jewelry must be removed. The magnetic field itself is not harmful, but it can interfere with some implanted devices. Mild sedation can be offered to people with claustrophobia. The exam is very low risk when safety rules are followed.

## Limitations of MRI
Image quality depends on the patient staying still; movement and metal implants blur or distort images. MRI takes longer and costs more than some other imaging tests. An MRI image alone often cannot determine the exact type or grade of a tumor; a biopsy is usually needed to confirm the diagnosis.

## Who interprets the results
A radiologist, a doctor specialized in reading medical images, analyzes the MRI and sends a signed report to the referring doctor, who discusses the findings with the patient. Follow-up scans may be recommended to monitor changes over time or check the effect of treatment.
