# Brain and Spinal Cord Tumors - Overview

## What is a brain tumor
A tumor forms when abnormal cells group together because of problems in the genes that control cell growth. Brain and spinal cord tumors develop in the central nervous system (CNS). Some tumors cause no symptoms. Others damage healthy cells, press on sensitive tissue, or block the flow of blood, fluid or signals to and from the brain.

## Benign vs malignant tumors
Benign (noncancerous) tumors usually do not spread and may need no treatment unless they cause problems; surgery is the most common treatment. Malignant (cancerous) tumors can invade nearby tissue and spread to other parts of the body, which makes complete surgical removal difficult.

## Primary vs secondary (metastatic) tumors
Primary tumors start in the brain or spinal cord. Secondary or metastatic tumors come from cancer elsewhere in the body that spreads to the CNS. In adults, secondary brain tumors are more common than primary ones.

## Common types of brain tumors
Meningiomas grow from the membranes (meninges) that cover the brain and spinal cord; they grow slowly and usually do not invade brain tissue. Pituitary adenomas form in the pituitary gland, which regulates hormones; most are benign and they become more common with age. Gliomas are the most common malignant brain tumors and start in glial cells that support nerve cells. Glioma subtypes include astrocytomas, ependymomas, oligodendrogliomas and optic gliomas. Embryonal tumors such as medulloblastoma occur mainly in children.

## Symptoms of brain tumors
Symptoms depend on the tumor's location, size and growth rate. Headache is the most common symptom. Other symptoms include seizures, nausea, vision problems, personality changes, speech difficulties, hormone disturbances, weakness or paralysis, trouble swallowing, loss of coordination and numbness.

## How brain tumors are diagnosed
Diagnosis starts with a medical history and neurological examination. MRI is the gold standard imaging test for CNS tumors; CT and functional MRI are also used. Other tests include blood and urine tests, EEG, lumbar puncture to examine cerebrospinal fluid, and biopsy, where a tissue sample is examined under a microscope. A biopsy is usually needed to confirm the exact tumor type.

## Treatment options
Treatment depends on the tumor type and severity. Neurosurgery is usually considered first, although some tumors are in locations where surgery is risky. Radiation therapy uses high-energy beams to destroy tumor cells. Chemotherapy uses medicines given in cycles. Tumor treating fields use low-energy electrical fields to stop cells dividing. Medicines also manage symptoms: anticonvulsants for seizures, steroids for swelling, and pain relievers. Targeted therapies act on specific genetic changes such as BRAF or IDH mutations.
